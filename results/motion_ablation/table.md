## Family: `distractor_overlap`

**Hard-overlap distractor.**  A bystander rides *on top of* the target (box offset (6,4) px, IoU 0.876) for `n_overlap` frames while the target keeps a clean template, so the geometry/size/motion costs are near-identical and the whole decision falls on appearance.  The bystander's descriptor is correlated with the target's by a swept cosine, while the target's own crop carries a fixed pose offset (`pose`, default 0.97): once the bystander is *more* similar to the template than the target's current crop, the best candidate is the wrong person and only `CostCfg.distractor_margin` can refuse.  After the window the bystander peels away, so a tracker that latched onto it loses the target and racks up false acquisitions.

## Family: `nonlinear_blackout`

**The decisive experiment.**  The target follows a curving path while the detector is blind for `gap` frames.  The target is still in GT (so those frames count as visible), but there is no observation: every reported box during the gap is *pure motion prediction*, and success needs IoU>=0.5 with the true box.  A constant-velocity model extrapolates the tangent and leaves the arc; a turn/acceleration model must stay on it.  This is the only family where prediction quality can change the target-centric outcome.

## Family: `unidentifiable_target`

**The requirement-(4) failure mode.**  The target walks, then HALTS, then stops being *identifiable*: for `n_blind` frames it is still detected at its true box but its descriptor is unusable (`mode='missing'`: no features at all, i.e. `appearance_valid=False`; `mode='orthogonal'`: a valid but orthogonal vector).  Meanwhile an unoccluded bystander with a clean, distinct descriptor rides the target's **stale prediction** -- the constant-velocity coast the halted target has fallen behind -- so geometry points at the bystander and appearance is the only cue that can refuse.  The bystander's similarity to the target's template is swept, and the target becomes identifiable again in the last `n_post` frames: the question is whether the tracker hands the track to the bystander (wrong-identity frames, no recovery) or refuses and re-acquires the true target.
